How it works

1
Zero-touch ETL in KNIME
Reads 108K+ POS rows, fills missing values automatically, and rolls everything into a lightweight monthly dataset so dashboards respond instantly.
2
Solve cold start (24 from 12)
Only one year of actuals existed, so I shifted 2025 back 12 months and reduced it by 8% to create a prior-year baseline. The report flags this as synthetic.
3
ARIMA beat linear regression
Linear regression scored R² 0.36 and missed the seasonal peaks and dips. ARIMA captured the retail cycle and gave a confidence range for the 2026 budget.
4
Tableau scenario planner
Sliders for price, volume, COGS and discount. Costs split 80% variable and 20% fixed. A Goal Seek engine calculates the price move needed to break even.

Key finding: the elasticity paradox

The Goal Seek engine first suggested a 1.7% price rise. But raising prices also cuts demand (modelled at −0.5x), so a 1.7% rise would not have closed the gap. Break-even needed 2.5%. A model that ignores elasticity would have missed the profit target.

Simulated profit variance before and after the elasticity-adjusted price increase.

Recommendations I made: replace static annual budgets with rolling 12-month forecasts, check every price change in the planner before it goes live, and use it in the monthly Sales and Operations meeting so finance, supply chain and marketing plan together.

Methodology & NLP Pipeline

1
Text Preprocessing in KNIME
Cleaned unstructured text using lowercasing, punctuation removal, stop-word filtering, tokenization, and lemmatization to prepare for machine learning.
2
Lexicon & Supervised Learning
Applied Hu & Liu's sentiment lexicons for explainable scoring, alongside Naïve Bayes and Logistic Regression algorithms for sentiment classification.
3
Fixing the 100% Positivity Bias
The initial model had 94% accuracy but ignored negative reviews entirely (Cohen's Kappa of 0.0). Applied SMOTE oversampling and macro-averaged F1 evaluation to correctly detect minority negative sentiments.

Key Findings & Insights

Keyword & Root Cause Extraction

Positive reviews highlighted product reliability and value (e.g., "easy", "perfect"). Negative lexicon extraction successfully isolated operational pain points like poor durability ("slow", "useless") and customer support issues ("return", "respond").

Verified Purchase Polarization

Analysis revealed that "Verified Purchases" (85% of the dataset) were significantly more polarized, showing more extreme positive and negative emotional expressions compared to non-verified reviews which clustered around neutral.

Time-Series Sentiment Trends

Detected a 12% spike in negative sentiment during holiday sale weeks, and a 10% dip correlating with a product recall, proving that sentiment tracking can preempt consumer backlash.

Business Implication

Relying on raw accuracy on an imbalanced dataset creates a false perception of customer satisfaction. By retraining the model to detect negative feedback (improving negative recall to 0.73), the business can now intervene on support, product quality, and delivery issues. "Only when a model can listen to all voices—not just the happy ones—can it truly drive customer-centric innovation."
